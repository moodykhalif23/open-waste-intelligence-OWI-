Waste operations, run on evidence

Know which bins need collecting today.

OpenWaste is a phone app for collectors and an operations dashboard for the people who plan the day. A photo at the bin becomes fill level, material mix, a route worth driving, and reporting you can put in front of anyone.

How it works

From photo to decision, in four steps.

Snap

A collector photographs the bin on the way past — no forms, no waiting for signal.

Understand

The photo is read for material and fill level. People are blurred before anything is stored.

Decide

Bin health ranks what needs collecting today, and the route is built around it.

Share

Aggregates — never raw photos or locations — flow to an open data feed for counties, researchers, and residents.

The problem

Collection runs on guesswork.

Trucks sweep fixed routes whether bins are full or empty. Overflow is discovered by complaint, days late. Recyclable value leaves the yard unweighed, and the crews doing the hardest work have the least data to show for it.

None of that is a sensor problem. A collector already stands in front of every bin, every day, holding a phone. That moment is the measurement — it has simply never been captured.

Routes that ignore reality

Fixed schedules send vehicles to empty bins and miss the ones already overflowing.

Overflow found by complaint

The first signal is a resident calling, long after the pile started building.

Value that leaks away

Recyclables leave unpriced, so nobody can prove what a day of work was worth.

The product

Two surfaces. One operation.

Nothing to integrate and nothing to wire up: a phone in the field, a browser in the office, and the same data underneath both.

The field app

For the collector, on the phone they already carry.

  • Photograph a bin in under twenty seconds
  • Works with no signal — reports queue and sync later
  • Scan the bin’s QR code, or let GPS place it
  • English and Kiswahili from the first screen
  • Delete any photo you took, no questions asked

The dashboard

For whoever plans the day — and answers for the month.

  • Bin health ranked: collect now, schedule soon, leave it
  • A route built for what you actually run
  • Recovered material priced against a local table
  • Cleanliness and carbon trends per area
  • Reports that export without a spreadsheet rescue

Trust

Hard lines, written into the code.

  • The platform never identifies a person. Not as a feature, not as an option.
  • Faces and bodies are blurred before a photo is stored, and the unblurred originals are destroyed within 72 hours.
  • A collector can delete any photo they took. It is erased, no questions asked.
  • Anything published is an aggregate — thin cells withheld, everything delayed a week. Raw photos and exact locations never leave.
  • A model ships only after clearing a fixed accuracy bar on data it has never seen, and reviewer corrections retrain it every week.
  • Every administrative action lands in an append-only audit trail.

Open source

Your data. Your hardware. No lock-in.

OpenWaste is Apache-2.0 and runs on hardware you control — a laptop, a mini-PC in an office, or a small server. Nothing phones home, there is no seat to buy, and if you ever walk away you keep every byte.
